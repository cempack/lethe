Protection

Eight layers. Each one does one thing.

Amnesia

No disk. Memory is locked out of swap and crash dumps, zero-filled on exit, and wiped by a separate guard if Lethe is killed.

Integrity

The Tails image is checked against the pinned Tails signing key before every start. Lethe's own files are root-owned and compared with a manifest.

Sandbox

QEMU and KVM inside bubblewrap: no home folder, no desktop socket, no D-Bus, no capabilities, no nested namespaces.

Memory privacy

Lethe, the machine and the wipe guard are closed to your other programs: no debugger, no reading their memory.

Network

The machine has no network of its own. A separate relay carries its traffic behind a kernel firewall: public internet only.

VPN before Tor

An optional WireGuard tunnel that only this session can use, and that is its only route. Your provider sees a VPN, the VPN only sees Tor.

Display

The machine never touches your desktop. Its picture reaches Lethe over a private link; no audio, no USB, no shared folders.

Sentinel

Watches this computer during a session and destroys it at the first sign of tampering. Hides it while your screen is recorded.

What it cannot do

  • Protect you from a compromised computer. If its kernel or firmware belongs to someone else, they see everything. For that threat, boot Tails from a USB stick on a machine you trust.
  • Hide that you use it. Lethe and the Tails image are on your disk. What you did in a session is not.
  • Hide Tor from your network, unless you use the VPN option or Tor bridges.

Install

Two commands.

$ gh repo clone cempack/lethe && ./lethe/install.sh
$ lethe

Then press Start Session. lethe doctor checks every protection on your machine, and ./install.sh --uninstall removes everything.
